The Move.inFormation group brings together researchers from behavioural ecology, movement science, engineering, and data science. Together we develop new methods to measure perception of and movement within dynamic energy landscapes. behaviour and explore how individuals and collectives interact with dynamic environments. Our work is highly collaborative and interdisciplinary. Fieldwork in remote environments, technological innovation in biologging, theoretical development, and comparative systems (human, robotic, natural) all play central roles in the research culture of the group.
