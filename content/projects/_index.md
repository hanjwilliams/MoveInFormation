The Move.inFormation research programme integrates theory, technology, and large-scale empirical systems to investigate how movement decisions emerge from the interaction between environmental energy and information. Our work centres on organisms that depend strongly on environmental energy to move efficiently, including systems that harness the wind both engineered and evolved. Complementary systems allow us to measure the energetic consequences of movement decisions while manipulating the availability of social information. 

How do animals perceive environmental energy? How do social cues shape decision-making? and How individual decisions scale up to collective movement patterns shaping ecology?
